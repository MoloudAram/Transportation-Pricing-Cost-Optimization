
# Transportation Pricing Methodology

## Overview

This document describes how transportation costs are calculated in the OptiChain model. The calculation is based on the physical characteristics of each product (volume and weight), the capacity of the vehicle (Khawar), the distance to the destination, and a set of fixed cost components.

The goal is to produce consistent cost estimates that can be used at different levels of detail — from individual products up to shipping groups and destination provinces or cities.

## Vehicle Capacity

The model uses a standard Khawar truck with the following fixed capacities:

- Volume capacity: 18.5 m³  
- Weight capacity: 2.0 tons  

These values are treated as constants and are applied to every product.

## Loading Quantity

For each product we calculate how many units can be loaded on one truck. Two limits are considered:

- Volume limit = 18.5 / product volume  
- Weight limit = 2.0 / product weight  

The final loading quantity is the smaller of the two values. If either volume or weight is zero (or missing), that constraint is ignored.

After the quantity is determined, the actual loaded weight is calculated as:

Loaded Weight = Final Quantity × Product Weight  

This value is then capped at the maximum truck weight of 2.0 tons.

The model also records which constraint was binding (volume or weight). This information is useful for understanding why certain products fill the truck faster than others.

## Distance

Distance is taken from the location master data.

- At province level the median distance of all cities in that province (within the same region) is used.  
- At city level the exact distance recorded for that city is used.

Using the median at province level reduces the impact of outlier routes.

## Cost Calculation

The cost of one full truckload is calculated as follows:

1. Base cost = Loaded Weight × Distance × Rate per ton-km  
2. Add fixed insurance amount  
3. Apply road transportation deduction (percentage)  
4. Apply loading & unloading surcharge (percentage)

In formula form:

Final Cost = (Loaded Weight × Distance × Rate + Insurance)  
             × (1 + Road Deduction %)  
             × (1 + Loading/Unloading %)

The commercial values of Rate, Insurance, and the two percentages are not published in this repository.

## Pricing Levels

The same logic is applied at four different granularities:

- Product × Province  
- Product × City (not generated by default because of size)  
- Shipping Group × Province  
- Shipping Group × City  

Shipping groups are created by aggregating products that share the same shipping group code. For group-level calculations the median loaded weight of the products inside the group is used.

## Output Files

The model produces the following files:

- `01_Product_Capacity.xlsx` – loading quantity, loaded weight and limiting factor for every product  
- `02_Group_Summary.xlsx` – number of products and weight statistics per shipping group  
- `03_Group_x_Province.xlsx` – cost matrix for every shipping group and province  
- `04_Group_x_City.xlsx` – cost matrix for every shipping group and city  
- `05_Product_x_Province_FULL.csv` – full product × province cost matrix  
- `05_Product_x_Province_Sample.xlsx` – sample of the above for easier viewing  

## Notes & Limitations

- Products with both volume and weight equal to zero are excluded.  
- Distances that cannot be converted to numbers are dropped.  
- The model currently assumes a single vehicle type.  
- No multi-stop or back-haul logic is included.  
- All costs are expressed in Iranian Rials; a separate column converts them to Tomans by dividing by 10.

## Confidentiality

This methodology is based on a real operational problem. The public version of the repository does not contain:

- Actual commercial rates  
- Customer or supplier names  
- Internal location codes that could identify the company  
- Any transactional shipment data  

Only the calculation logic and anonymized structure are shared.
